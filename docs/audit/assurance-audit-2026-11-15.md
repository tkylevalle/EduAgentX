# EduAgentX assurance audit, 15 November 2026

Scope: the whole repository on `main` at `adc450c`, checked against the stated
goal in [docs/blueprint/spec.md](../blueprint/spec.md): an Agent Learner learns a
governed competency, demonstrates it under adversarial assessment, and receives
verifiable evidence that others can rely on. The review follows the
code-review-and-quality five axes (correctness, readability, architecture,
security, performance). Every finding names the file that shows it.

## Verdict

The trust chain around the measurement is real engineering. The measurement
itself is not yet fit for the claim. The grader, the exam content, the agent's
context and the curriculum approval are placeholders, and the system's own
grading KPI is Red against the spec's Acceptance Floors. The custody chain also
has two gaps that weaken the strengths claimed for it: fingerprints are declared
by the agent, and credential status cannot be checked without our service.

Severity labels follow the review skill:

- **Critical:** the stated goal is false or unsupported until this is fixed.
- **Required:** a real gap that must be closed before the claim holds.
- **Optional:** worth doing; not required for the claim.
- **FYI:** context only.

## Questions the project cannot answer yet

1. What exactly does a credential claim, in one sentence, and which evidence supports each part of that sentence?
2. How was the grader validated against people's judgement, and how good is it on the real exam items?
3. Why does the KPI report zero safety false passes when its own dataset expects some?
4. Who reviewed the curriculum, other than its authors and the demo script?
5. What stops an agent from registering one configuration and running another?
6. How does a third party check suspension or revocation without calling our service?
7. What learning claim does the product make, and how is it measured?
8. What is the exam's threat model, given that the agent under test can be the attacker?
9. Which of the 23 services does the core claim need?
10. Who outside the team has used it, and what did they need it for?

## What holds up

These parts are real and were checked in code or on a real stack:

- Credentials are signed with Ed25519, and any change to a signed field fails verification (hero scenario 4).
- Gates fail closed. Missing evidence blocks; status that cannot be checked is reported as `INDETERMINATE`, never `VALID`.
- Evidence modes are labelled. Simulation credentials use their own issuer, and the Marketplace excludes what it cannot check.
- Exactly-once issuance and recovery work (scenario 3: issuer recovery in 1,672 ms, one credential).
- The pass rules (80% overall, 70% adversarial, zero critical violations) are enforced in one pure function and pinned per attempt.
- A changed declared configuration removes eligibility (scenario 6), within the limit described in D1.
- Swapping the grader, adding a sandbox, or giving the agent context does not require changes to signing, verification or matching.

## Order of work

1. One grader, measured on the production route, with labels made by people (A2, A3, A4, F4), and the result written into the credential (A11).
2. Validity tests that fail today: the parrot, paraphrased leaks, the first-option fallback agent (A9, A10, E2), run in CI (E1).
3. The sandboxed behaviour tier with repeated runs and a bound (A5, A7, A8).
4. Context and retrieval for the agent, then the learning-gain measurement (B1-B7).
5. Real curriculum review and verified sources (C1-C5).
6. Fingerprints observed by the platform, a signed status list and a published issuer key (D1-D3).
7. Evidence and documentation honesty: committed evidence, one verdict per report, a complete limitations record (E4, E5, E8).
